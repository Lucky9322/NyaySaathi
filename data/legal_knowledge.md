## 1. Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005

### What is RTI?
The Right to Information Act, 2005 (RTI Act) empowers every Indian citizen to request information from any public authority. It promotes transparency and accountability in government.

### Who Can File RTI?
- Any Indian citizen can file an RTI application.
- No reason needs to be given for seeking information.
- The applicant can be of any age, gender, or literacy level.

### What Information Can Be Sought?
- Records, documents, memos, emails, opinions, advices, press releases
- Circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers
- Data material held in any electronic form
- Information relating to any private body accessible by a public authority

### What Cannot Be Sought Under RTI?
- Information that affects national security, sovereignty, or integrity
- Information related to intelligence agencies (IB, RAW, etc.) except for corruption/human rights violations
- Information received in confidence from foreign governments
- Cabinet papers, Council of Ministers records (before decision + 20 years)
- Personal information with no public activity or interest
- Information that would harm competitive position of third parties
- Information held in fiduciary capacity

### RTI Application Process

#### Step 1: Identify the Public Information Officer (PIO)
- Every public authority has a designated PIO
- Central Government departments: CPIO (Central Public Information Officer)
- State Government departments: SPIO (State Public Information Officer)
- Send application to the PIO of the relevant department

#### Step 2: Write the Application
- Address to the PIO of the specific department
- Clearly state the information sought (specific questions)
- Include your full name, address, and contact details
- No need to state why you want the information

#### Step 3: Pay the Fee
- Central Government: ₹10 application fee
- Mode: Cash, DD, Banker's Cheque, or IPO (Indian Postal Order)
- BPL (Below Poverty Line) card holders: Free (attach BPL card copy)
- State governments may have different fees (usually ₹10-₹50)

#### Step 4: Submit the Application
- In person at the PIO's office
- By registered post/speed post
- Online via RTI Online Portal: https://rtionline.gov.in (Central Government)
- Many states have their own online portals

#### Step 5: Receive Response
- The PIO must respond within **30 days** of receiving the application
- If information concerns life/liberty: within **48 hours**
- If sent to wrong PIO: transferred within 5 days, total time still 30 days
- If no response: file First Appeal

### RTI Timelines
| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Normal information | 30 days |
| Information affecting life/liberty | 48 hours |
| Information from third party | 40 days |
| First Appeal decision | 30 days |
| Second Appeal/CIC hearing | 90 days (target) |

### RTI First Appeal
- If PIO doesn't respond or response is unsatisfactory
- File with First Appellate Authority (FAA) within **30 days** of receiving PIO response (or 30 days after deadline if no response)
- FAA must decide within **30 days** (extendable to 45 days with reasons)

### RTI Second Appeal / Complaint
- If First Appeal is unsatisfactory
- File with Central Information Commission (CIC) or State Information Commission (SIC)
- Within **90 days** of FAA order
- CIC Website: https://cic.gov.in
- Complaints also filed with CIC/SIC for PIOs who don't have FAA

### RTI Penalties
- PIO can be penalized ₹250/day (max ₹25,000) for:
  - Refusing to receive application without reason
  - Not furnishing information within time limits
  - Malafidely denying information
  - Giving incorrect/incomplete/misleading information
  - Destroying information subject to RTI request

### Important RTI Sections
- **Section 2(f)**: Definition of "information"
- **Section 2(h)**: Definition of "public authority"
- **Section 3**: Right of citizens to information
- **Section 6**: Request for obtaining information
- **Section 7**: Disposal of request
- **Section 8**: Exemption from disclosure
- **Section 19**: Appeal procedure
- **Section 20**: Penalties

### Useful RTI Resources
- RTI Online Portal (Central): https://rtionline.gov.in
- Central Information Commission: https://cic.gov.in
- India Code - RTI Act: https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2082/1/200527.pdf

## 2. Filing FIR / Police Complaints

### What is an FIR?
A First Information Report (FIR) is a written document prepared by police when they receive information about a cognizable offence. It sets the criminal law in motion.

### Cognizable vs Non-Cognizable Offences
- **Cognizable**: Police can arrest without warrant and investigate without magistrate's order (murder, robbery, rape, kidnapping, dacoity)
- **Non-Cognizable**: Police need magistrate's order to investigate (assault without grievous hurt, cheating below threshold, public nuisance)
- **Both types**: Right to get a copy of FIR / complaint registered

### Who Can File an FIR?
- The victim themselves
- Any witness to the offence
- Any person on behalf of the victim
- Police officer who learns of the offence

### What Information to Include in FIR?
1. Date, time, and place of the incident
2. Names of accused (if known) or description
3. Names of witnesses (if any)
4. Nature of offence committed
5. Details of property stolen/damaged (if applicable)
6. Brief narration of the incident in sequence

### FIR Filing Process
1. Go to the **police station** having jurisdiction over the area where the crime occurred
2. Report to the **Station House Officer (SHO)**
3. Give a statement — police will reduce it to writing
4. Read the FIR before signing (or get it read to you)
5. Get a **free copy of the FIR** (your legal right under Section 154 CrPC)
6. Note the FIR number for future reference

### If Police Refuse to Register FIR
**Step 1:** Request Superintendent of Police (SP) or Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP)
**Step 2:** Send complaint by post to SP/DCP
**Step 3:** File a complaint before the Judicial Magistrate under Section 156(3) CrPC
**Step 4:** File complaint with State Human Rights Commission or National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) if rights are violated

### Zero FIR
- Can be filed at ANY police station regardless of jurisdiction
- Police must transfer it to the appropriate police station
- Useful in emergencies when the crime area's police station is far

### Section 154 CrPC — Key Rights
- Police MUST register FIR for cognizable offences
- Refusal to register FIR is punishable
- Complainant entitled to free copy of FIR

### Online FIR / Complaints
- Many states have online FIR portals for non-cognizable offences
- Delhi: https://delhi.gov.in/services
- Maharashtra: https://www.mahapolice.gov.in
- Cybercrime: https://cybercrime.gov.in (Mandatory for cyber offences)

### Important Legal Sections
- **Section 154 CrPC**: Obligation to register FIR
- **Section 155 CrPC**: Non-cognizable offences information
- **Section 156 CrPC**: Police officer's power to investigate
- **Section 173 CrPC**: Completion of investigation report
- **Section 482 CrPC**: High Court's inherent powers (quash FIR if frivolous)

## 3. Consumer Protection Act, 2019

### Consumer Rights
Every consumer has the right to:
1. **Safety**: Protection against hazardous goods and services
2. **Information**: Right to be informed about quality, quantity, price
3. **Choice**: Right to access variety of goods at competitive prices
4. **Hearing**: Right to be heard in consumer disputes
5. **Redressal**: Right to seek redress against unfair trade practices
6. **Consumer Education**: Right to acquire knowledge and skills

### Who is a Consumer?
A person who:
- Buys goods for consideration (payment)
- Uses goods for personal use (not commercial resale)
- Hires/avails services for consideration
- The beneficiary of such goods/services

### Types of Consumer Complaints
- **Defective goods**: Product not as described, unsafe, faulty
- **Deficiency in services**: Medical, banking, insurance, education, transport
- **Unfair trade practices**: False advertising, misleading claims
- **Restrictive trade practices**: Limiting competition
- **Overcharging**: Charging more than MRP

### Consumer Dispute Redressal Forums
| Forum | Claim Amount |
|-------|-------------|
| District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (DCDRC) | Up to ₹50 Lakhs |
| State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (SCDRC) | ₹50 Lakhs to ₹2 Crore |
| National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) | Above ₹2 Crore |

### Filing Consumer Complaint
**Online (recommended):** https://consumerhelpline.gov.in or https://edaakhil.nic.in
**Offline:** File at the District Consumer Commission office

**Documents Required:**
- Copy of invoice/bill/receipt
- Warranty card (if applicable)
- Correspondence with the company
- Photos/evidence of defect
- Medical/expert reports (if applicable)

**Filing Fee:**
- Up to ₹5 Lakhs: ₹200
- ₹5-10 Lakhs: ₹400
- ₹10-20 Lakhs: ₹500
- And so on (nominal fees)

**Time Limit:** File within **2 years** of the date of cause of action (when problem occurred)

### Consumer Helpline
- National Consumer Helpline: **1800-11-4000** (toll-free)
- SMS: **8800001915**
- Website: https://consumerhelpline.gov.in

### Mediation
Under the 2019 Act, Consumer Commissions can refer disputes to mediation before hearing. This is faster and less formal.

## 4. Fundamental Rights (Part III of Constitution)

### Article 14 — Right to Equality
- Equality before law
- Equal protection of laws
- No discrimination by the State

### Article 15 — Prohibition of Discrimination
- No discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth
- Exceptions for women, children, and socially/educationally backward classes

### Article 16 — Equality of Opportunity
- Equal opportunity in matters of public employment
- No discrimination in government jobs

### Article 19 — Right to Freedom
1. Freedom of speech and expression
2. Freedom to assemble peaceably
3. Freedom to form associations
4. Freedom of movement throughout India
5. Freedom to reside and settle anywhere in India
6. Freedom to practice any profession or occupation

### Article 20 — Protection Against Arbitrary Conviction
- No ex-post-facto laws (cannot be punished for act not illegal when done)
- No double jeopardy (cannot be tried twice for same offence)
- No self-incrimination (cannot be compelled to be witness against oneself)

### Article 21 — Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
- No person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law
- This includes: right to livelihood, health, privacy, education, speedy trial, legal aid

### Article 21A — Right to Education
- Free and compulsory education for children aged 6-14 years
- Right to Education Act, 2009 operationalizes this

### Article 22 — Protection Against Arrest and Detention
- Right to be informed of grounds of arrest
- Right to consult a lawyer of your choice
- Must be produced before magistrate within 24 hours
- Cannot be detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate's order

### Article 23 — Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings
- Prohibition of forced labour, begar (unpaid labour)
- Violation punishable by law

### Article 24 — Prohibition of Child Labour
- No child below 14 years employed in factories, mines, or hazardous occupations

### Article 25-28 — Freedom of Religion
- Freedom of conscience and religion
- Freedom to manage religious affairs
- Freedom from paying taxes for religious instruction

### Article 32 — Right to Constitutional Remedies
- Right to move Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights
- Dr. Ambedkar called this "Heart and Soul of the Constitution"
- Writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto

## 5. Key Legal Writs

### Habeas Corpus ("You may have the body")
- Used when a person is illegally detained
- Court orders the detaining authority to produce the person
- Can be filed by the detained person or anyone on their behalf
- Important for illegal arrests, wrongful detention

### Mandamus ("We command")
- Orders a public official/government body to perform a legal duty
- Cannot be issued against the President or Governors
- Used to compel RTI responses, register FIR, etc.

### Certiorari
- Higher court quashes/reviews order of lower court or tribunal
- Used when lower court acts without jurisdiction or violates natural justice

### Prohibition
- Higher court prevents lower court from exceeding its jurisdiction
- Issued BEFORE the lower court's decision (unlike Certiorari)

### Quo Warranto ("By what authority")
- Questions the authority by which a person holds a public office
- Used against illegal occupation of public office

## 7. Free Legal Aid

### Who is Entitled to Free Legal Aid? (Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987)
- Women and children
- Members of SC/ST communities
- Victims of mass disasters, ethnic violence, or floods
- Persons with disabilities
- Persons in custody
- Persons whose annual income is below ₹3 Lakh (varies by state)

### How to Access Free Legal Aid
- Contact District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) in your district
- Call NALSA helpline: **15100**
- Visit: https://nalsa.gov.in
- Lok Adalats (People's Courts) for pre-litigation settlement
